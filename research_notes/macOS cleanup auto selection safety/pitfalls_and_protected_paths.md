# Pitfalls and Protected Paths: What a macOS Cleanup Tool Must Never Touch

Scope: macOS 13 Ventura to macOS 26 Tahoe (2022–2026). Older behaviour is flagged. Source-quality legend: **[Apple]** = Apple primary documentation; **[EL]** = Howard Oakley, eclecticlight.co (expert, primary testing); **[Forum]** = Apple Community / MacRumors / Jamf anecdote (corroboration only); **[Vendor]** = cleaner-app vendor blog (has a commercial interest); **[Snippet]** = taken from a search-result summary, page not fetched in full, so verify before quoting. **Single-source** marks claims with only one source.

## 1. Which items under ~/Library/Caches are actually state, or cause problems when deleted?

### Takeaway
The "Caches" name does not guarantee the data can be thrown away. iCloud Drive's `com.apple.bird` / CloudDocs data, CloudKit caches, and caches belonging to running apps are tied to sync state or live processes. Apple's own route for freeing cache space is CacheDelete (the `deleted` daemon) and Safe Mode, not deleting folders by hand. Even Apple's route frees much less than people expect.

### Cited Findings
- **CacheDelete handles purgeable caches for the OS.** Purgeable space has four parts: user caches managed by CacheDelete, local iCloud Drive copies, local iCloud media and app content, and Time Machine snapshots. CacheDelete starts after login and runs mostly in user mode. — [EL: Will flushing caches free up disk space? (2023)](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/09/22/will-flushing-caches-free-up-disk-space/)
- **Even Apple's Safe Mode cache flush frees little.** Apple says Safe Mode "deletes some system caches, including font caches and the kernel cache, which will be created again automatically as needed." Oakley notes that since Big Sur, kernel caches have been replaced by kernel collections, so clearing them is unlikely to free much space. He says deleting caches "will invariably achieve considerably less" than the maximum reported. — [EL 2023](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/09/22/will-flushing-caches-free-up-disk-space/)
- **The figures can get worse after a flush.** CacheDelete's updated figures after Safe Mode "may be worse than those it started with," though more accurate. — [EL 2023](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/09/22/will-flushing-caches-free-up-disk-space/)
- **What `com.apple.bird` is.** It is the cache folder of the `bird` daemon, which syncs iCloud Drive. Users report it growing to 86 GB, 251 GB, and in one anecdote over 1.6 TB. — [Vendor: MacPaw](https://macpaw.com/how-to/fix-bird-process-mac); [Forum: MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/very-large-files-in-com-apple-bird.2240150); [Forum: Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255888674) [Snippet]
- **Deleting bird/CloudDocs can break iCloud sync.** One user deleted the bird cache and the CloudDocs folder and reported "nothing is working." Several users say turning iCloud Drive off and on in System Settings emptied `com.apple.bird` safely, in one case down to under 200 MB. — [Forum: Apple Community 255888674](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255888674); [Forum: Apple Community 6603335](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/6603335) [Snippet; anecdotal]
- **CloudKit caches also grow very large.** An Apple Community thread reports a "massive cache attributed to CloudKit." — [Forum](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254021261) [Snippet; I found no Apple guidance on whether deleting it is safe]
- **The Mail Envelope Index is a rebuildable index, but rebuilding is disruptive.** Moving the Envelope Index files out of `~/Library/Mail/V10/MailData` while Mail is quit forces a full reindex, which can take a long time. One user said it did not fix their problem. Reports describe this as a deliberate troubleshooting step with a backup made first, not as routine cleanup. Note that the index lives under `~/Library/Mail`, not under Caches. — [Forum: Apple Community 255486046](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255486046); [UCSD IGPP wiki](https://ucsd-igpp.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/igppwiki/pages/7046224/How+to+Rebuild+and+Reindex+Apple+Mailbox) [Snippet]
- **Rebuilding a mailbox deletes local copies.** A Mail "rebuild" deletes the local copies of messages and downloads them again from the server. Messages stored only "On My Mac" have no server copy. — [Vendor: MacPaw](https://macpaw.com/how-to/rebuild-mailbox-mac); [Forum: "On my Mac" folders – emails disappeared](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/251045293) [Snippet]
- **A Mac cleaner developer's own never-delete list (corroboration).** It names: caches of an app that is open or mid-update ("can break a running or updating app"); wallpaper and system media caches (they blank the Settings UI "for almost no reclaim"); product-named `Caches/…` folders, which "may hold clipboard history or model caches"; `…/tmp` folders, which may hold drafts or checkpoints; and large `.db`/`.sqlite` files, which are "often primary user data." — [Vendor: Mole, "What Mac cleaners should never delete"](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md) [Vendor; single-source for the wallpaper claim]
- **Icon caches.** Removing `com.apple.dock.iconcache` and `com.apple.iconservices` under `/private/var/folders` is a common fix for broken Dock icons. One commenter said it also removed some icons that had been working. — [mjtsai: Clearing the icon services cache in Mojave (2019)](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2019/02/01/clearing-the-icon-services-cache-in-mojave/); [gist](https://gist.github.com/ismyrnow/e92c6010cda9325b2d8811387a05f224) [Snippet; pre-Ventura]

### Inferences
- A cleaner should never delete `~/Library/Caches/com.apple.bird`, `~/Library/Caches/CloudKit`, `com.apple.cloudd`-related caches, or anything else tied to iCloud or FileProvider sync. If the goal is to shrink them, send the user to Apple's own controls: turn iCloud Drive off and on, use "Remove Download", or turn on Optimise Mac Storage.
- Treat `com.apple.*` caches as hands-off by default. Apple's daemons and CacheDelete manage them, the space they free is small and quickly re-created, and deleting them while their owners are running is the main way things break.
- Safe-to-auto-select caches should be limited to known third-party cache folders whose owning app is not running, and the tool should re-check that state immediately before deleting.
- The size a tool shows should be labelled as a maximum. Oakley's tests show that real reclaim is "considerably less."

### Gaps
- I found no Apple or Oakley documentation that specifically covers deleting `~/Library/Caches` content for Safari, Messages, Photos, Music/TV, Maps/GeoServices, App Store (`com.apple.appstore`), or FileProvider. Evidence is limited to forum anecdotes and vendor blogs. I found no reliable incident reports specifically about the Photos, Music, or Maps caches.
- I found no documented re-indexing or re-sync incident with an Apple citation for CloudKit cache deletion.

## 2. Sandboxed containers, Group Containers, TCC-protected data, Full Disk Access, TCC.db and Keychains

### Takeaway
`~/Library/Containers/<bundle-id>` is the sandboxed app's entire private home folder: its documents, settings, and databases, not just its cache. Since macOS 14, containers are protected, and since macOS 15, Group Containers are too. These containers, the TCC databases, and Keychains must be on a hard never-touch list. Deleting any of them causes data loss that cannot be undone, or a reset of privacy permissions.

### Cited Findings
- **A container is the app's whole world.** A Container is "effectively the hierarchy of files and folders that the app has exclusive and unrestricted access to." Containers "shouldn't be removed or tampered with unless you're sure you know what you're doing," and "If in doubt, leave Containers well alone." A document saved in a container's Documents folder "will then be gone for ever" if the container is deleted. — [EL: What are all those Containers? (Aug 2024)](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/08/05/what-are-all-those-containers/)
- **Containers survive uninstall.** macOS does not delete a Container when its app is uninstalled. A new one is created if the app is reinstalled. Group Containers carry extra risk because several apps may share them. — [EL 2024](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/08/05/what-are-all-those-containers/)
- **Protection by macOS version.** In Sonoma (14), Containers and Daemon Containers are protected: macOS ties an app's code signature to its Container, and "If a different app tries to access the contents of that Container, then the user is asked whether to allow that access." In Sequoia (15), Group Containers gained SIP-based protection that limits access to member apps. — [EL 2024](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/08/05/what-are-all-those-containers/)
- **Apple's statement.** "App data containers that the system creates for apps that have the App Sandbox capability offer System Integrity Protection." "In macOS 15 and later, app group containers also offer System Integrity Protection… Any app not in the app group that attempts to access locations protected by an app group or app data container, results in a prompt to the user to authorize access." — [Apple Developer: Protecting local app data using containers on macOS](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/protecting-local-app-data-using-containers)
- **Possible change in macOS 27.** A third-party site claims that in macOS 27, access to other developers' containers is denied by default without a prompt. — [macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/macos-27-app-data-containers-access-denied-operation-not-permitted-fix-2026/) [Single-source, non-Apple, unverified]
- **Group Containers hold shared suite data.** For example, Office and other suites keep shared data here. A cleaner vendor's own guide warns that deleting the whole folder can break apps or lose data. — [Vendor: CleanMyMac blog](https://cleanmymac.com/blog/group-containers-folder-mac); [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md) [Snippet for CleanMyMac]
- **Location of the TCC databases.** The master database is `/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/TCC.db`. The user database is `~/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/TCC.db`. Ad hoc signature data is kept in `AdhocSignatureCache` next to each. Deleting the master database can only be done from Recovery. Oakley calls it the "nuclear option": "Only when all else fails and you're desperate should you consider deleting the TCC database or its entire folder." Deleting only the master can leave a problem in the user database. — [EL: Should you reset its database or delete it? The woes of TCC (Feb 2023)](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/02/09/should-you-reset-its-database-or-delete-it-the-woes-of-tcc/)
- **What a TCC reset does.** `tccutil reset` relies on the databases themselves to delete settings, and it can leave items behind. An Apple Developer Forums thread reports `tccutil reset SystemPolicyAllFiles` removing items from the GUI while TCC.db still listed them. — [EL 2023](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/02/09/should-you-reset-its-database-or-delete-it-the-woes-of-tcc/); [Apple Developer Forums 724635](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/724635) [Snippet]
- **What Full Disk Access is.** Full Disk Access is the TCC service `kTCCServiceSystemPolicyAllFiles`. "TCC first checks whether kTCCServiceSystemPolicyAllFiles applies," and if it does, the per-location checks are skipped. Tested on macOS 26.4. — [EL: Privacy: How locations are protected (Apr 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/04/20/privacy-how-locations-are-protected/)
- **Locations protected by TCC on macOS 26.4.** These are `~/Documents`, `~/Downloads`, `~/Desktop`, removable volumes, iCloud Drive, third-party cloud storage (FileProvider), network volumes, and Time Machine backups. Oakley says this is not a complete official list. On Full Disk Access: "If you have any doubts whatsoever, don't install that app, and certainly don't give it Full Disk Access." — [EL: Privacy: protected folders (Apr 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/04/07/privacy-protected-folders/)
- **iCloud Drive and FileProvider storage are hard to reason about.** They "are more difficult to investigate," have no GUI control, and intent (for example, the user choosing a file in an Open dialog) does not override their protection. — [EL Apr 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/04/20/privacy-how-locations-are-protected/)
- **Personal data stores.** A cleaner developer lists Photos library packages and their internal databases, Mail data (including envelope indexes and attachments), Messages databases and attachments, and Notes and Voice Memos as never-delete because they are irreplaceable. It also lists `~/Library/Mobile Documents` (iCloud Drive) and `~/Library/CloudStorage/…`, where a local delete "can propagate" to other devices. — [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md)
- **Keychains, and how sync spreads deletions.** If a user turns off iCloud Keychain and does not keep the data on at least one device, the data is deleted from the device and from iCloud. One user disabled sync, deleted items locally, and re-enabled sync. The empty Mac state was pushed to iCloud and wiped the logins on the iPhone as well. A deleted login keychain can only be recovered by re-adding an older copy of the file. — [Forum: Apple Community 252645764](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252645764); [Forum: Apple Community 255896087](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255896087) [Snippet; anecdotal]
- **Credentials and policy.** Keychains, browser login databases, token stores, TCC databases, configuration profiles, MDM payloads, and security-agent state are all listed as never-delete. — [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md)
- **A cleaner on Tahoe, followed by lost folder access (Oct 2025, macOS 26.0).** After a CleanMyMac scan deleted "some background items," the user was denied access to Documents and Downloads in Finder. Experienced responders recommended a Time Machine restore or erasing the Mac. Cause was not confirmed. — [Forum: Apple Community 256174524](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256174524)

### Inferences
- Hard never-touch list for this area: `~/Library/Containers/*` (except possibly a container's `Data/Library/Caches` subfolder, and only for a third-party app that is not running), `~/Library/Group Containers/*`, `~/Library/Daemon Containers/*`, `~/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/`, `/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/`, `~/Library/Keychains/`, `/Library/Keychains/`, `~/Library/Mail/`, `~/Library/Messages/`, `~/Library/Safari/`, `~/Pictures/*.photoslibrary`, `~/Library/Mobile Documents/`, `~/Library/CloudStorage/`, and `~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/`.
- Since macOS 14 and 15, touching another app's container from a cleaner triggers a user prompt or fails. Mass deletion in these locations will either fail, flood the user with prompts, or (with Full Disk Access) quietly do damage. A tool should not rely on Full Disk Access to get past these protections.
- A tool needs a TCC-denial (EPERM) path that treats "permission denied" as "skip and report," not "retry with escalation."

### Gaps
- Apple's term "Data Vault" (the restricted storage behind Mail, Messages, Safari and similar data) did not appear in any source I fetched. I found no citable Apple page that lists which folders are Data Vaults.
- I found no source that documents the exact user-visible effects of deleting the user `TCC.db` on macOS 13–26, beyond Oakley's "last resort" warning.

## 3. SIP and the Signed System Volume: protected paths and what tools must not attempt

### Takeaway
Since macOS 11, `/System`, and therefore the whole OS, sits on a cryptographically sealed, read-only Signed System Volume. SIP also protects `/usr` (except `/usr/local`), `/bin`, `/sbin`, `/var`, and Apple's preinstalled apps. A cleaner must never attempt writes there and must never ask users to disable SIP.

### Cited Findings
- **Paths SIP protects, per Apple.** "System Integrity Protection includes protection for these parts of the system: /System, /usr, /bin, /sbin, /var, Apps that are pre-installed with the Mac operating system." "Paths and apps that third-party apps and installers can continue to write to include: /Applications, /Library, /usr/local." Changes to protected parts are allowed only for Apple-signed processes with special entitlements, such as Apple software updates. — [Apple Support 102149: About System Integrity Protection](https://support.apple.com/102149)
- **Exceptions inside `/usr`.** All directories in `/usr` except `/usr/local` are restricted to the system. — [Apple Developer (archived): SIP Guide – File System Protections](https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/documentation/Security/Conceptual/System_Integrity_Protection_Guide/FileSystemProtections/FileSystemProtections.html) [older doc]
- **How exceptions are defined.** Protected paths and their exceptions are listed in `/System/Library/Sandbox/rootless.conf` and `/System/Library/Sandbox/Compatibility.bundle/Contents/Resources/paths`. — [Wikipedia: System Integrity Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Integrity_Protection) [Snippet; secondary]
- **The Signed System Volume.** "macOS 11 or later adds strong cryptographic protections to system content with a signed system volume (SSV)." It has "a kernel mechanism that verifies the integrity of the system content at runtime and rejects any data—code and noncode—without a valid cryptographic signature from Apple." The seal is a Merkle-tree root hash over every byte. On Apple silicon, the bootloader verifies the seal before handing control to the kernel. The SSV cannot be turned off while FileVault is on. — [Apple Platform Security: Signed system volume security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/secd698747c9/web) [Snippet]
- **An unsealed system cannot be resealed by the user.** Once unsealed, the system cannot be resealed except with a macOS installer or `asr`. — [Jamf: What's new in macOS Big Sur security](https://www.jamf.com/blog/whats-new-in-macos-big-sur-security/) [Snippet; secondary]
- **Sonoma and Sequoia extended SIP-backed protection** to app Containers and Group Containers (see section 2). — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/protecting-local-app-data-using-containers); [EL 2024](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/08/05/what-are-all-those-containers/)

### Inferences
- Never-touch paths: `/System/**`, `/usr/**` except `/usr/local`, `/bin`, `/sbin`, `/private/var/**` except named user-temp subpaths handled with care (section 4), `/Applications` entries for Apple-bundled apps (Safari, Mail, and so on), `/Library/Apple/**`, and the Preboot, Recovery and VM volumes.
- Tools must not instruct users to disable SIP or authenticated root (`csrutil disable`, `csrutil authenticated-root disable`), must not try to mount the system volume read-write, and must not delete system language files. On macOS 11 and later, this last action is impossible anyway because of the SSV.
- "Operation not permitted" errors under these paths are expected and should be suppressed from user-facing "failed items" counts, or shown as "protected by macOS."
- Apple's support page lists `/var` as protected without exceptions, which is a simplification. Write access in fact exists for parts of `/private/var`, such as `/private/var/folders` per user, which is handled in section 4. A tool should not take the existence of a write path as permission to delete.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the current `rootless.conf` contents for macOS 26, so there is no cited, exact list of `/private/var` exceptions.

## 4. /private/var/folders: is manual deletion safe?

### Takeaway
`/private/var/folders/xx/<hash>/{C,T,0}` holds per-user caches (C), temporary files (T), and other per-user state for many running processes. Clearing it while logged in is risky. Community reports describe both fixes (Dock icons) and breakage (boot problems, missing QuickLook). The safe, Apple-aligned route is to restart, or start in Safe Mode, which flushes these caches under OS control.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple engineer, 2008.** The folder is the standard parent directory for per-user temporary directories, and "lots of processes use those directories, not just Xcode." Deleting the whole directory is advised against. — [lists.apple.com xcode-users, Jul 2008](https://lists.apple.com/archives/xcode-users/2008/Jul/msg00347.html) [Snippet; pre-2022, but the structure is unchanged]
- **Forum advice: be selective.** Do not delete `/private/var/folders` wholesale. Delete only temporary directories you understand. — [Forum: Apple Community 3757828](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/3757828) [Snippet]
- **Leave the `zz` folder alone.** One MacRumors user reported that deleting the whole `folders` directory caused a boot problem. — [Forum: MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/cannot-open-app-its-damaged-or-incomplete.1987438) [Snippet; anecdotal, older macOS]
- **A Dock problem fixed by clearing, which came back later.** A Dock with question marks on every icon and nothing launching was fixed by clearing the contents (not the folders themselves) of the affected user's folder, then rebooting. A Jamf admin reported the problem returning weeks later. — [Jamf Nation](https://community.jamf.com/t5/jamf-pro/run-away-var-folders/m-p/37435); [Forum: Apple Community 7106331](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/7106331) [Snippet]
- **QuickLook.** A user who deleted QuickLook files could not restore them by reinstalling and was advised to restore from Time Machine. — [Forum: Apple Community 6619152](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/6619152) [Snippet; anecdotal]
- **The space is real but managed.** `$DARWIN_USER_CACHE_DIR` can hold several GB. — [ctrl.blog: The missing gigabytes of disk space on my Mac](https://www.ctrl.blog/entry/darwin-user-cache-gigabytes/) [Snippet]
- **Safe Mode is Apple's mechanism.** Safe Mode deletes some system caches, including font caches. CacheDelete refreshes its services in Safe Mode, making several passes that can last 20 seconds or more. — [EL 2023](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/09/22/will-flushing-caches-free-up-disk-space/); [EL: Safe mode, caches and CacheDelete (May 2023)](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/05/03/safe-mode-caches-and-cachedelete/)

### Inferences
- A cleaner should never auto-select anything under `/private/var/folders`. At most it could offer a guided "Restart / Safe Mode" action. If it ever deletes there, it should be limited to the current user's `T/` (temporary) items older than a cutoff that no running process has open. `0/`, `C/`, and `zz` should be off-limits, because `C/` holds live caches such as icon services, fonts, and LaunchServices-related data.
- `/private/tmp` is likewise cleared on reboot and is not worth the risk.

### Gaps
- I found no Apple support article from 2022 to 2026 that explicitly says "do not delete `/var/folders`." Apple's guidance is implicit (Safe Mode and restart). Oakley's articles found here do not directly address manual `/var/folders` deletion.
- I found no well-sourced, recent (macOS 13–26) incident report about Notification Center breaking after `/var/folders` deletion. That claim is folklore here.

## 5. Time Machine local snapshots and APFS purgeable space

### Takeaway
Local snapshots are counted as "purgeable." Deleting them can free real space, but macOS's purgeable accounting is unreliable. Finder and Storage settings misreport it, and macOS sometimes fails to purge snapshots it should. Deleting the most recent snapshot can affect the next backup, and running `tmutil delete` against a backup disk destroys real backup history.

### Cited Findings
- **Snapshots are purgeable.** When APFS snapshots arrived, "they too were added to purgeable space," and "Time Machine automatically purges its own snapshots when they're more than 24 hours old." The `tmutil` man page says "local snapshots are considered purgeable and may be removed at any time by deleted(8)," while Disk Utility's help suggests they are not purgeable. Apple's documentation contradicts itself here. — [EL: Aren't snapshots purgeable? (Aug 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/08/24/arent-snapshots-purgeable/)
- **Purgeable accounting misleads.** Finder and the Storage settings "give figures that are incorrect, by omitting purgeable space." In tests, 94.8 GB of snapshot data showed sometimes as Used and sometimes as Purgeable. On at least two occasions macOS "failed to recognise the purgeability of the huge snapshot… and refused to purge it," and a 12.89 GB copy failed. Disk Utility is "the only GUI tool in macOS that may report space consistently." — [EL Aug 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/08/24/arent-snapshots-purgeable/)
- **Space release is delayed.** In one test, space was released only about 29 minutes after the files were deleted. Time Machine's CacheDelete fails to report purgeable space even when it is available. — [EL: Why can purging snapshots fail? (Aug 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/08/27/why-can-purging-snapshots-fail/) [Snippet]
- **Which snapshots can be deleted.** "you can safely delete all except the most recent snapshot, when each snapshot is accompanied by a backup." Keep the most recent one, because Time Machine may need it for snapshot diffing, and it is the fallback if the backup disk is unavailable. Deleting snapshots may make the next backup larger. Since Catalina, Time Machine relies mainly on FSEvents. In High Sierra and Mojave it relied on snapshot diffing. Snapshots from other backup utilities can probably be deleted, but check that vendor's documentation. — [EL: Which snapshots could you delete? (Feb 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/02/which-snapshots-could-you-delete/)
- **Apple's thinning tool.** `tmutil thinlocalsnapshots <mount_point> [purge_amount] [urgency]` is the controlled way to thin snapshots, rather than deleting them by hand. — [EL: Take control of snapshots (Sep 2026)](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/09/09/take-control-of-snapshots/) [Snippet]
- **Never run `tmutil delete` on a backup destination.** Running it against a backup destination removes real backup history. Leave startup-volume snapshots alone unless there is a demonstrated need. — [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/how-to-delete-local-time-machine-snapshots-mac) [Vendor; single-source]
- **Purgeable space includes iCloud local copies.** "Available" space already includes purgeable space. Its four parts are CacheDelete user caches, local iCloud Drive copies, local iCloud media and app content, and Time Machine snapshots. — [EL 2023](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/09/22/will-flushing-caches-free-up-disk-space/)

### Inferences
- A cleaner that deletes snapshots and then reports "X GB freed" may be claiming space macOS would have freed on demand anyway, or space that does not appear for about 30 minutes. Reports should measure free space with the same API before and after, label snapshot reclaim as "purgeable → free," and never add it to "junk" totals.
- Auto-selection should never include snapshots. If they are offered at all, keep the newest one, exclude `com.apple.os.update-*` and other system snapshots, never touch backup-destination snapshots, and prefer `tmutil thinlocalsnapshots`.

### Gaps
- None of the sources fetched cover system (`com.apple.os.update-*`) snapshots or SSV snapshots, so I have no citation for "must not delete." This is a strong inference given that the SSV depends on snapshots ([Apple Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/secd698747c9/web) notes the SSV uses APFS snapshots for update rollback).

## 6. Application Support "orphans": why an uninstalled app's leftovers are not proof the data is disposable

### Takeaway
Leftover containers and Application Support folders often hold the only copy of user documents, databases, licences, or data shared with other apps from the same vendor. macOS deliberately keeps Containers after uninstall so that a reinstall picks up where the user left off.

### Cited Findings
- **Containers are deliberately kept.** macOS does not delete an app's Container on uninstall. A document saved inside it is lost "for ever" if the container is removed. — [EL 2024](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/08/05/what-are-all-those-containers/)
- **"Looks disposable" paths that often are not.** `Library/Application Support/…` may hold app databases, offline packs, or licence state. `Group Containers/…` may hold shared suite data. Vendor parent folders should be left alone while any product from that vendor remains installed. Group containers shared across a suite are still needed by the other apps. — [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md)
- **Incident: an uninstaller deleted personal files.** A user who uninstalled another app (Locklizard) with CleanMyMac reported it deleted "all my files and personal settings." — [Forum: Apple Community 5877946](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/5877946) [Snippet; ~2014, older]
- **Incident: an "aggressive" clean removed library content.** A Native Instruments user reported a recent CleanMyMac update removing files from sound libraries and making them unusable. — [Native Instruments Community](https://community.native-instruments.com/discussion/comment/224216) [Snippet only; the page could not be fetched, so unverified]
- **Incident: a cleaner broke DEVONthink.** DEVONthink's forum has a thread titled "Anyone have problems with CleanMyMac new version." — [DEVONtechnologies forum](https://discourse.devontechnologies.com/t/anyone-have-problems-with-cleanmymac-new-version/82317) [Title only, not fetched]
- **Incident: Firefox data lost.** A MacRumors user reported recent Firefox data gone after CleanMyMac deleted files, with other apps restorable only from an older backup. — [Forum: MacRumors 989155](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/cleanmymac-deleted-system-files.989155/) [Snippet; ~2010]

### Inferences
- An app's absence from `/Applications` should never be enough on its own to auto-select its leftover data. Signals that must also hold before a tool even suggests removal: no other app with the same Team ID or bundle-ID prefix is installed; the folder is not a Group Container; the folder holds no `.sqlite`, `.db`, or user-document types; and the item is not an App Store app that is merely offloaded or about to be reinstalled. Even then, items should go to Trash or quarantine, never be deleted outright.
- Leftovers should be shown for review, not auto-selected. This matches Oakley's requirement that every cleaning action be reversible (section 8).

### Gaps
- I found no Apple documentation on App Store reinstall behaviour, such as whether reinstalling restores a deleted container from iCloud. This is not sourced.
- I found no reports specific to licence files under Application Support beyond the vendor blog's general claim (single-source).

## 7. Removing language files (Monolingual and similar), Mail/Photos/Messages attachments, and iOS backups as the only copy

### Takeaway
On macOS 11 and later, system localisations sit on the sealed SSV and cannot be stripped. Stripping localisations from third-party apps is at best a small saving, carries a signature risk, and is discouraged by experts. Attachments held by Mail, Messages and Photos, and local iOS backups, may be the only copy of the data. Apple warns that a deleted backup cannot be recovered.

### Cited Findings
- **What code-signing rules say about removed translations (2015).** "The code signing rules also allow language translations to be removed, but if they are present they must be unmodified." This was written about El Capitan's Gatekeeper, after Sparkle's localisation symlinks caused a signature failure. — [furbo.org (Craig Hockenberry), Jul 2015](https://furbo.org/?p=1818) [older]
- **Contrary evidence on modified bundles.** Modifying any file in a signed bundle invalidates the seal. `codesign -v -vvv --strict --deep` shows "a sealed resource is missing or invalid" with "file missing" lines. On macOS 14 and later, `gktool` can also be used to check. — [Apple Developer Forums: Resolving Gatekeeper problems](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/706379) [Snippet]. This is an unresolved conflict: the default resource rules may tolerate a missing `.lproj`, but a strict check reports missing files. Behaviour on macOS 13–26 is not confirmed by any source fetched.
- **Monolingual itself gives no warnings.** Its README gives no warnings about code signatures, SIP, App Store apps, or system files. It requires an admin to approve a privileged helper. — [GitHub: IngmarStein/Monolingual](https://github.com/IngmarStein/Monolingual)
- **Oakley's position.** He personally avoids "stripping OS X components like language localisations without good reason" and requires any cleaning to be reversible. — [EL: Why you shouldn't use cleaning or housekeeping apps (May 2016)](https://eclecticlight.co/2016/05/28/why-you-shouldnt-use-cleaning-or-housekeeping-apps/) [older]
- **System localisations cannot be changed on macOS 11 and later.** The SSV rejects any unsigned system content (see section 3). — [Apple Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/secd698747c9/web)
- **Local iOS backups.** Backups are stored at `~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup/`. Apple warns: "After you delete a backup, you can't recover it." Apple's supported path is Finder → General → Manage Backups. — [Apple Support 108809](https://support.apple.com/108809)
- **iCloud backups are separate.** Backup folder names are random and do not identify the device. iCloud backups are separate from Mac backups, so deleting a Mac backup does not touch iCloud, and the Mac copy may be the only copy if iCloud Backup was off. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2020/01/15/how-to-delete-iphone-backups-mac-macos-catalina/); [Apple Community 253098051](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253098051) [Snippet]
- **Attachments.** Mail data and attachments, Messages databases and attachments, and Photos library internals are irreplaceable personal data. — [Vendor: Mole](https://mole.fit/blog/what-mac-cleaners-should-never-delete.md) [Vendor; I found no Apple-sourced warning in this pass]

### Inferences
- Localisation stripping should not be a feature. The SSV blocks it for system files, App Store and notarised apps risk failing strict signature checks, and the saving is small.
- iOS backups must never be auto-selected. They can be listed with age and size, but deletion should go through Finder's Manage Backups or need explicit per-backup confirmation that shows the device name and date.
- `~/Library/Mail/**/Attachments`, `~/Library/Messages/Attachments`, and anything inside a `.photoslibrary` package belong on the hard never-touch list.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2022–2026) documented incident of Monolingual or any localisation stripper breaking an app's signature on macOS 13–26.
- I found no Apple support article warning against deleting Messages or Mail attachment folders directly.

## 8. Documented incidents with cleaner apps, and warnings from Apple-community experts and Oakley

### Takeaway
Reports of cleaners causing damage are mostly forum anecdotes with unclear cause, including a 2025 macOS Tahoe case. The best-documented formal action against a cleaner was about deceptive marketing (the MacKeeper scareware class action), not data deletion. Expert consensus (Oakley, high-ranked Apple Community contributors) is that macOS manages caches itself and that cleaners risk irreversible damage.

### Cited Findings
- **MacKeeper class action (2014–2015).** Filed in May 2014 in the W.D. Pennsylvania (Yencha v. ZeoBIT). It alleged that MacKeeper falsely flagged security and performance problems to push a $39.95 purchase. ZeoBIT offered a $2M settlement without admitting fault. 513,330 US buyers were covered. — [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/942082/mackeeper-customers-can-file-a-claim-to-get-their-money-back.html); [KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/lifestyle/mobile/apple/matthew-wilson/mackeeper-to-pay-2-million-in-class-action-settlement/); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/424118/mackeeper-buyers-ask-for-refunds-in-droves-following-lawsuit.html) [Snippet; pre-2022, about deceptive claims rather than data loss]
- **CleanMyMac on macOS 26.0 (Oct 27, 2025).** A user on an M1 MacBook Pro running macOS 26.0 deleted "background items" via a CleanMyMac full scan and then lost Finder access to Documents and Downloads. Causation was not confirmed. Top contributors responded: leroydouglas called it "As close to malware as you can get and still call it an app"; BobTheFisherman said such apps "use system resources while providing no benefit"; John Galt said uninstalling doesn't undo the damage and recommended erasing the Mac; Grant Bennet-Alder said macOS already handles cleaning. — [Apple Community 256174524](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256174524)
- **Other CleanMyMac damage reports.** Lost settings, Finder permission problems, and damage needing repair are reported in Apple Community threads. Several involve pirated copies or unclear causes. — [Apple Community 255526751](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255526751); [Apple Community 254029024](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254029024); [mac-help.com](https://www.mac-help.com/threads/all-files-suddenly-disappeared-after-using-cleanmymac-x.230558) [Snippet]
- **Note on source quality.** Many search results about "files deleted by CleanMyMac" are data-recovery vendor marketing pages (Remo, iBeesoft, DoYourData) and are not independent evidence.
- **Oakley's criteria for any cleaner (2016).** Documentation that "details exhaustively what each of its functions does" in terms of what it moves or removes; easy reversal of every function; security claims backed by a good track record; immediate updates after each macOS release; and no adware or scareware. Without an undo you can be left with "a sick Mac, trying to re-install OS X and various broken apps." He said reviewing such tools for MacUser meant "it would take me a couple of weeks to get my Mac working properly again afterwards." — [EL May 2016](https://eclecticlight.co/2016/05/28/why-you-shouldnt-use-cleaning-or-housekeeping-apps/) [older but still cited]
- **macOS manages its own storage.** Optimised storage makes most cleaning tools unnecessary. — [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/optimized-storage-mac-cleaning-tools); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/805940/do-you-need-a-cleaner-app-for-your-mac/) [Snippet]
- **Even a cleaner's own blog says this.** Modern macOS cleans up after itself far better than it used to, with developer caches as a real exception. — [Vendor: Mole, "Do you need a Mac cleaner"](https://mole.fit/blog/do-you-need-a-mac-cleaner) [Snippet]

### Inferences
- The recurring failure modes in incidents are: (a) deleting items the user did not understand, such as "background items" or uninstaller leftovers; (b) no undo; (c) acting on shared or vendor data during an uninstall. A safe tool's design answers map to each: show what will happen and why, quarantine instead of deleting, and keep a protected-path and shared-data guard.

### Consolidated never-touch list (synthesis of sections 1–8; each entry traces to the citations above)
- **System and SIP/SSV:** `/System/**`, `/usr/**` except `/usr/local`, `/bin`, `/sbin`, `/private/var/**` (except carefully scoped per-user temp, and never by default), Apple-bundled apps in `/Applications`, `/Library/Apple/**`, and system or update APFS snapshots.
- **Privacy and credentials:** `~/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/`, `/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/`, `~/Library/Keychains/`, `/Library/Keychains/`, configuration profiles and MDM state.
- **App data:** `~/Library/Containers/*` (whole), `~/Library/Group Containers/*`, `~/Library/Daemon Containers/*`; Application Support folders whose vendor still has any installed app.
- **Personal libraries:** `~/Library/Mail/`, `~/Library/Messages/`, `~/Library/Safari/`, `*.photoslibrary`, Music/TV libraries, `~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup/`, Notes and Voice Memos stores.
- **Sync and cloud:** `~/Library/Mobile Documents/`, `~/Library/CloudStorage/`, `~/Library/Caches/com.apple.bird`, `~/Library/Caches/CloudKit`.
- **Live and temporary state:** caches of running apps, `/private/var/folders/**` (offer a restart instead), staged macOS installers or updates, and package-manager stores while in use.
- **Review only, never auto-selected:** Time Machine local snapshots (keep the newest), iOS backups, orphaned Application Support, developer caches such as DerivedData or node_modules, and localisations.

### Gaps
- I found no formal regulator action or vendor acknowledgement of a data-loss bug in CleanMyMac, MacKeeper, or other major cleaners for 2022–2026. Evidence is forum anecdotes.
- I found no Apple support article that explicitly warns against third-party cleaner apps. Apple-side warnings come only from community contributors, not Apple staff.
- I did not reach Objective-See or TidBITS coverage specifically on cleaners in this pass.
